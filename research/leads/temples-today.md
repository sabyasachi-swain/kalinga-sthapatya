# Present Status of the Five Temples: Research Leads

This document compiles factual research leads on the present status of the five principal Kalinga temples: **Parasuramesvara Temple**, **Mukteshvara Temple**, **Lingaraja Temple**, **Jagannath Temple (Puri)**, and **Sun Temple (Konark)**. It addresses ongoing worship, administrative and statutory management, admission rules for non-Hindus, national and international protection status, and official visiting facts.

## B) Conflicts

### 1. Cause of Collapse and Desertion of the Sun Temple, Konark
*   **View A (Structural/Foundation Failure):** Percy Brown (*Indian Architecture*, 1959, p. 107) asserted that the sanctuary was never completed and failed during construction: *"There are fairly clear proofs that it was never quite completed as before the ponderous stones that formed the portion of the tower, could be put into position, the foundation began to give way... It was, however, a magnificent failure."* James Fergusson (1876) similarly attributed collapse to foundation failure in marshy subsoil.
*   **View B (Slow Decay and Neglect Following Desecration):** Debala Mitra (ASI, 2003, p. 12) rejects the unfinished thesis, noting that the temple was consecrated and fell into neglect after desecration: *"Forsaken by the presiding deity, the deserted temple fell into utter neglect. This must have been the main cause of the decay, which was possibly accelerated by the fall of the capping stones."* K. S. Behera (2005, pp. 91–92, 101) demonstrates that the temple was complete, consecrated, and had active storekeepers (*bhandara-adhikari*) recorded in 14th-century inscriptions; UNESCO experts in 1987 verified that *"the foundations are sound"*.

### 2. Date and Circumstances of Worship Cessation at Konark
*   **View A (Desertion by 1626 documented by foreign eyewitness):** Mahmud b. Amir Wali (*Bahr-al-asrar*, May 1626, cited in Behera 2005, pp. 95–96) explicitly recorded: *"These days Hindus do not worship the Sun god. For this reason Konark has also been deserted."*
*   **View B (Desecration and deity removal under Khurda Raja in 1628):** The *Madala Panji* (cited in Behera 2005, p. 103) records that Maharaja Narasimhadeva of Khurda visited Konark on his 9th Anka (March 17, 1628) and removed the deity (*Maitraditya-virancideva*) to the Niladrimahotsva shrine within the Jagannath Temple at Puri due to the depredations of Mughal Subedar Baqar Khan.

### 3. Legal and Administrative Jurisdiction at Centrally Protected Living Temples
*   **View A (Exclusively Central AMASR Act 1958 Authority):** The Archaeological Survey of India exercises statutory power over centrally protected monuments of national importance under the *Ancient Monuments and Archaeological Sites and Remains Act, 1958* (Act 24 of 1958), restricting alterations, new constructions, and unauthorised works within 100m prohibited and 200m regulated zones. In 2022, the Union Ministry of Home Affairs flagged the Odisha State Government's proposed *Shri Lingaraj Temple Ordinance, 2020*, stating that certain provisions encroached upon central authority under the AMASR Act, 1958.
*   **View B (State Religious Endowments / Temple Board Governance):** Day-to-day religious rituals, sevayat rights, properties, and worship arrangements are governed under state legislation: the *Odisha Hindu Religious Endowments Act, 1951* (Orissa Act 2 of 1952) for Lingaraja Temple, and the *Shri Jagannath Temple Act, 1955* (Orissa Act 11 of 1955) for Jagannath Temple, Puri. Under Article 26 of the Constitution of India, religious denominations maintain rights to manage internal religious affairs, creating a dual-custody framework alongside the ASI.

### 4. Judicial Suggestions vs. Customary Entry Rules at Jagannath Temple, Puri
*   **View A (Judicial Suggestion of Permitting Non-Hindus):** In July 2018, during the hearing of *Mrinalini Padhi v. Union of India*, a Supreme Court of India bench suggested that temple management could consider allowing non-Hindus into the temple subject to regulatory conditions and dress codes.
*   **View B (Customary and Statutory Bar Under Temple Tradition):** The Shree Jagannath Temple Managing Committee, the Gajapati Maharaja of Puri, and servitor bodies rejected the suggestion, asserting that long-standing custom codified under the *Shri Jagannath Temple Act, 1955* and its Record-of-Rights strictly reserves entry for practicing Hindus, while non-Hindus are permitted darshan of Patitapavana at the Singhadwara or during the annual Ratha Yatra.
