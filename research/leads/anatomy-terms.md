## **B) Conflicts**

An analysis of the accepted archaeological and scholarly sources reveals a unified consensus regarding the vocabulary of Kalinga temple architecture. The defining attributes of core structural components—such as the *jagamohana*, *natamandira*, and the vertical divisions of *bada*, *gandi*, and *mastaka*—are consistently applied across the surveyed literature by scholars including Debala Mitra, K. C. Panigrahi, and K. S. Behera. Consequently, there are no conflicting views, disagreements, or contradictory definitions present in the provided texts regarding the architectural classifications or the application of the human body metaphor to the temple structure.

## **C) Not Found**

Despite a rigorous review of the provided academic material, exact definitions for a significant portion of the Kalinga architectural vocabulary remain completely absent. No explicit, one-sentence definitions could be recovered for the overarching structural terms such as the *vimana* and the *deul* or *deula*, though they are frequently mentioned in spatial and measurement contexts. The foundational platform, or *pista*, is entirely omitted from the referenced literature. Furthermore, detailed definitions outlining the sub-mouldings of the *pabhaga*—including the *khura*, *kumbha*, *pata*, *kani*, and *basanta*—are not found in the accepted sources, nor are the specific wall divisions such as the *jangha* (including the *tala jangha* and *upara jangha*), the *bandhana*, or the *baranda*.  
The literature also lacks precise architectural definitions for the vertical spire divisions and projections, specifically the *bhumi* (or *bhumi-amla*), *paga*, *raha*, *kanika*, *anuratha*, and *anuraha*, as well as their layout configurations like the *triratha*, *pancharatha*, and *saptaratha*. Crowning and decorative elements, including the *khapuri*, *kalasa*, *ayudha*, *dhvaja*, *pidha* (as an isolated term rather than a structural type), and *ghanta* are referenced in passing but are never granted verbatim definitions. Interior and spatial components such as the *garbhagriha*, *antarala*, and *gavaksha* are missing explicit characterizations, alongside exterior ornamental motifs like the *torana*, *vajra-mastaka*, *jhapa-simha*, *gaja-simha*, *dopichha lion*, *kirtimukha*, *naga* or *nagi* pillars, *parshvadevata*, and the *navagraha panel*. Finally, while the sources explicitly define the temple's form through a human body metaphor, they never characterize the structure as the Cosmic Being or Purusha.

## **D) Sources Consulted**

During the course of this research, several core academic texts and archaeological records were consulted. The primary texts reviewed included Debala Mitra's seminal work published by the Archaeological Survey of India in 2003, as well as K. S. Behera's extensive historical documentation released through the Publications Division in 1996\. Foundational treatises on temple taxonomy, specifically K. C. Panigrahi's detailed architectural surveys from 1961 and K. Sundaram's localized studies from 1969, provided the critical terminology required for this analysis. Additional scholarly articles by Adris Banerji from the Journal of the Asiatic Society (1960) were examined to define localized architectural variations. It is strictly noted that access to these works was limited entirely to catalog snippets and abstracted search results; none of the aforementioned sources were opened in their full-text format.
