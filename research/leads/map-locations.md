# **Geospatial and Administrative Delineation of Kalingan Temple Architecture**

The accurate mapping of Kalingan temple architecture for interactive educational platforms requires precise administrative, geospatial, and protective status data. Establishing the exact location of monumental sites such as Parasuramesvara, Mukteshvara, Lingaraja, Jagannath Puri, and Konark relies on interpreting official archaeological documentation while navigating historical shifts in district boundaries and transcription anomalies in digital records. A rigorous analysis of Archaeological Survey of India (ASI) and Indira Gandhi National Centre for the Arts (IGNCA) documentation reveals highly specific boundary definitions that dictate how these monuments should be represented geospatially.

## **Administrative Topography of Bhubaneswar Monuments**

The spatial representation of Bhubaneswar's early and mature temple sites requires an understanding of their localized placement within the modern Old Town area. For the Parasuramesvara temple, official site documentation locates the structure precisely within "Village (village code) Badu Sahi. Post : Old Town. Via: Bhubaneswar. Tehsil: Bhubaneswar. District: Khurda. State : Orissa."1. The administrative placement within the Khurda district is consistent across modern records for Bhubaneswar's central monuments.  
Similarly, the Mukteshvara temple is firmly situated within this modern administrative framework. Site reports define its location as "Muktesvara Temple Complex, Old Town. Post : Old Town. Via: Old Town. Tehsil: Bhubaneswar. District: Khurda. State : Orissa."2. Relative geographic positioning provided in the documentation notes that it is located "on the right side of the road leading from Bhubaneswar to Puri."2.  
The Lingaraja temple complex shares this primary administrative designation, recorded as being located "Via: Old Town, Bhubaneswar. Tehsil: Bhubaneswar District: Khurda. State : Orissa."3. Further localized geographic context for mapping the Lingaraja node places it "on the right bank of the river Gangua and on the left side of the Lingaraja temple road"4.  
An analysis of the raw geospatial coordinates provided in the IGNCA/ASI documentation highlights the necessity for careful data validation before inputting parameters into an interactive map. The coordinates for the Mukteshvara temple are recorded clearly as "Longitude: 85°50'26”E Latitude: 20°14'33”N Elevation: 52 ft."2. However, the corresponding documentation for the Parasuramesvara temple contains evident transcription or optical character recognition anomalies, listing the geospatial data as "LONGITUDE 85050'97”E LATITUDE 20015'36”N ELEVATION 42 ft."1. Such raw coordinate strings cannot be parsed by standard mapping software without manual correction to standard degrees, minutes, and seconds formatting.

## **Protected Boundaries and Polygons**

For an interactive map, determining whether a temple should be represented as a single point (node) or a bounded area (polygon) depends on the exact legal definition of the protected site. The ASI's "Centrally Protected Monuments" list provides critical instructions regarding the scope of these sites.  
In the locality of Bhubaneswar, the "Parsurameswar temple" is protected as a singular entity5. In contrast, the spatial footprint for Mukteshvara is explicitly defined to include and exclude specific features, listed as the "Mukteswar temple with its minor shrines but excluding the Murich Kunda"5. This requires the exclusion of the Murich Kunda tank from the protected polygon on the map. The Lingaraja complex requires a broad polygon encompassing multiple structures, as it is protected as the "Lord Lingraj Temple with all the minor temples in the compound"5.  
For monuments outside the Khurda district, the administrative and protective parameters change. Both the Jagannath Temple and the Sun Temple are located in the Puri district. The ASI defines the Puri site as "Locality: Puri, District: Puri" and designates the protective scope as the "Shri Jagannath Temple and subsidiary shrines together with adjacent area"5. The Konark site, listed under "Locality: Konark, District: Puri", features the most expansive spatial definition in the dataset, protecting "The Ancient Monument of the Black pagoda and the ruins and remains of all ancient edifices, images, structures, basements, pillars carvings, walls, gateways, etc."5. This mandates a wide mapping boundary capturing the entire architectural footprint rather than just the standing jagamohana.

## **B) Conflicts**

A distinct contradiction exists in the administrative classification of Bhubaneswar across different chronological sources. Historical scholarship traditionally mapped the city within the Puri district. K.C. Panigrahi's historical overview records this geographical layout, stating: "District furi : Bhubaneswar, Udayagiri hills, Ranpur, Sisupal- garh."7. Conversely, contemporary governmental and archaeological databases reflect modern district reorganization. The modern Archaeological Survey of India and IGNCA documentation explicitly places the monuments of Bhubaneswar in a different administrative zone, noting the location for sites like the Parasuramesvara temple as "Tehsil: Bhubaneswar. District: Khurda."1.

## **C) Not found**

Extensive queries of OpenStreetMap (OSM) data failed to return exact node or way URLs for the Parasuramesvara, Mukteshvara, Lingaraja, Jagannath Puri, and Konark temples. While search queries successfully retrieved generic text strings and highway routing data pointing toward these sites, the precise structural XML nodes or way URLs representing the temple buildings themselves could not be located in the accessed dataset. Furthermore, the requested "H2 temples" are entirely absent from all provided scholarly and archaeological texts; no architectural, historical, or epigraphic reference to monuments bearing this classification could be identified. Finally, while the ASI "Centrally Protected Monuments List" details the locality and protective scope of the structures, the specific alphanumeric ASI monument listing numbers (such as N-OR-1) assigned to each site are not printed in the accessed digital text.
